# Scenario 4: The whole company

**Who:** all departments, managers, agents per area and maybe a "company" agent that knows policies and processes.

**A typical day**
- Each department has its channels and agents (scenario 3 repeated).
- There are cross-department channels (`#sales-finance`), where the sales agent asks the finance agent for the status of a payment.
- Leadership receives a weekly summary per department, written by the agents.
- The new employee talks to the onboarding agent.

**What becomes mandatory**
- **Governance:** who creates agents, who connects systems, who approves publishing.
- **Audit:** a record of everything each agent did and at whose request. MeshAgent highlights "fully traceable" [9].
- **Strong identity:** each agent with its own key and permissions, as in Buzz [1].
- **Cost per area:** panel by department, person and agent.
- **Integration** with the company login and internal systems.

**Possibilities**
- **4a:** expand department by department from scenario 3. The video and the reports cited indicate this works better than everyone at once.
- **4b:** a single platform from the start, with central rules.
- **4c:** each department with its own tool and a "bridge" agent that links them all.

**Risks:** wrong permission exposed to many people, cost out of control and resistance from people.

**Makes sense when:** at least one department (scenario 3) has already proven value.
