# The idea

## Where the idea came from

A video by a content creator shows a workspace where humans and agents work together.

What the video shows:

- A Slack-like interface: channels on the left, conversation on the right.
- Humans and agents as **members of the same channel**. In the example, three people talk to the same agent at the same time.
- **Bring your subscription:** each person connects the Claude Code and Codex that run on their own machine. This does not consume platform credit, but the agent goes offline when the machine shuts down.
- **Agent talks to agent:** one person's Claude Code asks the lead agent for something, and it replies, with no human in the middle.
- Each channel has **apps** (dashboards and kanbans built by the agents themselves), **automations** (for example, every 6 hours: research, create, ask for approval, publish and measure), **files** and **threads** that can be closed without being deleted.
- Profiles for each member with role, goals and "voice"; credit usage visible per person, per room and per agent.
- Central thesis: until now AI has been **"single-player"**, each person with their own isolated system. The next step is **"multiplayer"**, with people and agents from several owners working in the same space.

**What we want to explore:** a concept system where the messages from **Telegram bots**, **agents** and **users** arrive and stay visible in one place, and think about how this would serve **companies** and **games**.

## The thesis under discussion: single-player × multiplayer

**In favor of the thesis**
- The market is moving this way. Block launched Buzz, an open space where humans and agents share channels [1][2][3]. A field report from Anthropic describes a Claude working with several people at once in Slack, with its own credential and memory [5]. The same summary says YC started asking for "shared agent sessions" that the team watches, redirects and hands off [5].
- Avoids duplicate work: today each person builds their own integrations and skills (argument from the video and from [4]).
- Makes visible what today stays hidden in separate terminals and bots.

**Against the thesis, or cautions**
- It can become "yet another Slack": noise, too many messages, nobody reads.
- The video itself admits all of this could already be assembled before, just with more technical effort.
- The category is still forming [5]. Buzz declares itself unfinished [1][3].

**Open question:** is the main gain collaborating (several people) or observing (one person watching several agents)? In the INEMA case, today the second seems to weigh more.
